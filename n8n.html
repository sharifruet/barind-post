4. Don't immediately scrape everything

I strongly recommend this priority:

Level 1 — RSS

RSS → n8n → Article URL

Level 2 — Official API

API → n8n → Article

Level 3 — Website extraction

Website → extractor → article

RSS/API is much cleaner.

Also, you should check each publisher's terms/robots rules and copyright/licensing position. Rewriting an article does not automatically give you the right to republish it. A safer model is to use the source for discovery and create genuinely original reporting/summaries, with attribution and links where appropriate.

6. Deduplication is extremely important

Suppose:

Newspaper A: "Government announces new education policy"

and

Newspaper B: "New education policy announced by government"

These may be the same story.

You don't want:

Article 1
Article 2
Article 3
Article 4

on your site.

I'd eventually implement:

Exact duplicate

URL/hash comparison.

Near duplicate

Embedding similarity.

For example:

Article A embedding
        ↓
Vector comparison
        ↓
Article B = 94% similar
        ↓
Same story

PostgreSQL + pgvector is sufficient initially.

9. Don't let AI invent facts

This should be one of the central design principles.

Your AI pipeline should distinguish:

SOURCE FACT
     ↓
VERIFIED FACT
     ↓
GENERATED PROSE

For example, if source says:

The minister said the project will cost Tk 500 crore.

The AI shouldn't produce:

The project will definitely create 50,000 jobs.

unless that information exists in the source.

I'd actually store fact provenance.

fact_id
article_id
fact
source_reference
confidence

This makes your system much more reliable.

One important recommendation

I would also avoid calling the output a "rewritten newspaper article." Architecturally, make it an original news synthesis system: collect source material, extract/verify facts, combine multiple sources where possible, and generate a new article with source attribution. That is both technically better and much safer from a copyright/editorial standpoint.
